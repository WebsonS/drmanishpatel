## 3. Gap analysis: what the clinic's new site has and lacks

Strengths over the set:
- Surgeon-authored, risk-first content (35 posts) with consistent answer blocks and clean schema; competitors mostly have thin service pages or social-only presence.
- Two clinic locations (Margao + Panaji), a two-city coverage that no single competitor above has with surgeon-owned pages.
- 11 treatment pages with `MedicalProcedure` schema, FAQ markup, breadcrumbs and `llms.txt`: stronger structured data than every competitor inspected.
- Faculty roles (Wockhardt Institute of Aesthetics 2011; IAAPS AESURG 2022; FADS World Congress 2023) are unmatched trust assets if stated factually.

Gaps:
1. **Existing rankings sit on the old WordPress URLs** (the old homepage already appears for the generic query); a bad cutover forfeits them (Audit C1).
2. **No local-pack presence evidence**: GBP not linked; off-site NAP drift.
3. **No dedicated service pages** for blepharoplasty, breast lift/reduction, otoplasty, PRP, lip fillers, mommy makeover although the posts exist; competitors rank for these with service pages.
4. **No cost/finance content**: price-sensitive queries ("hair transplant cost in Goa", "rhinoplasty cost Goa") are the highest-volume commercial queries in India and are owned by aggregators. NMC bars promotional pricing and offers, but an educational "what determines cost" explainer without figures or packages is permitted and answers the intent (confirm wording with the clinic's compliance reading; no "starting from", no discounts).
5. **No medical-tourism / NRI / out-of-state planning content** (Goa draws visitors from Mumbai, Bengaluru, Pune, the Gulf and the UK). Content on travel planning, recovery stay length and follow-up is allowed and unserved.
6. **No Hindi/Konkani content**; Goa searches often mix English with Hindi/Konkani terms (low priority, test later).
7. **Third-party citations**: Practo, Justdial, Sulekha and listicle sites carry outdated or conflicting data.

## 5. Strategic implications

1. **Defend before attack.** Protect the old URLs' equity through a clean 301 map (Roadmap Phase 0). The generic "plastic surgeon in Goa" position is the asset.
2. **Win the weak SERPs first.** Rhinoplasty, blepharoplasty, otoplasty, breast reduction, facelift and gynecomastia-in-Goa have weak local competition; rank them with deep surgeon-authored pages plus supporting posts.
3. **Hair transplant is a long game.** Do not fight price pages; build the surgeon-led cluster (FUE/FUT, hairline design, women, beard/eyebrow, PRP, monsoon planning, aftercare video) and rely on GBP and the Panaji/Margao pages.
4. **Local pack through GBP**: two verified listings, accurate categories, services, weekly educational posts, FAQs answered on the site (public GBP Q&A is being phased out), photos of premises (no patient images).
5. **Authority via faculty and societies**: request profile/citation links from IAAPS, FADS, AESURG, Wockhardt and hospital pages; PR for the educational events the doctor teaches at (e.g. relevant conferences in Goa such as the Global Masters in Rhinoplasty, March 2027, if the doctor is involved; mention only factual participation).
6. **Differentiate on compliance as a trust signal**: a clear "How to verify your surgeon" guide (NMC register lookup, degree check) positions the clinic as the transparent option versus competitors that use "best" claims and galleries. Competitors' over-claiming is a long-term risk for them; the clinic can avoid it.
