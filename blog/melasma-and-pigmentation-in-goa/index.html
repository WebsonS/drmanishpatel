Melasma and Pigmentation: Why the Sun in Goa Matters

Short answerMelasma is a long-term tendency to brown patches, driven by sunlight, heat, hormones and genetics. It can be controlled and lightened but usually not cured, so daily sun protection is the foundation, with creams, peels or lasers used carefully on top.

Not all dark patches are melasma

Sun spots, marks left after acne or injury, and some skin conditions can look similar. Each responds differently, so the first step is a correct diagnosis, sometimes with a closer look under a special lamp or dermatoscope.

Why it keeps coming back

  • Ultraviolet and visible light, including light through windows
  • Heat, which matters in a coastal climate
  • Hormonal changes, including pregnancy and some contraceptives
  • Irritation from harsh products, which can darken the skin further

What a treatment plan usually includes

  • Broad-spectrum sunscreen applied generously and reapplied, plus a hat outdoors
  • Prescription creams that reduce pigment production
  • Chemical peels in a series, chosen for your skin type
  • Laser or light treatment in selected cases, at conservative settings

Aggressive treatment can make pigmentation worse on Indian skin, so progress is gradual and checked at each visit.

Realistic expectations

Lightening usually takes months. Maintenance continues after the patches fade, because the tendency remains. Pregnant women need a modified plan, as some creams are not suitable.
