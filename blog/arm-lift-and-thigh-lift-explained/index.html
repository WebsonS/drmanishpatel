Arm Lift and Thigh Lift: When Skin Is the Problem

Short answerAn arm lift (brachioplasty) or thigh lift removes loose, hanging skin that exercise cannot tighten, most often after major weight loss. The trade-off is a longer scar along the inner arm or thigh, so it is considered once weight has been stable for some time.

Who asks about these operations

Most are people who have lost a large amount of weight, through diet, bariatric surgery or sustained lifestyle change, and are left with skin that hangs from the upper arm or inner thigh. Others notice slackness with age or after pregnancy. Skin that has been stretched for a long time loses its ability to recoil, and no cream or exercise changes that.

Is it fat or is it skin?

This decides everything. If the problem is mainly fat with reasonably firm skin, liposuction alone may be enough. If skin hangs and folds, removing it is the only way to reduce it. Many people have both, and the plan combines methods. A pinch test and examination in standing and raised-arm positions tell the surgeon far more than any photograph.

The arm lift

An incision is made along the inner or back of the arm, sometimes from the elbow up to the armpit. Excess skin is removed and the wound closed in layers. The scar is the cost of the operation, and it can be visible in sleeveless clothes, particularly in the first year when it is red. In some people with limited laxity a shorter scar is possible, but that depends on examination.

The thigh lift

Inner thigh lifts typically use an incision in the groin crease, sometimes extending down the inner thigh. Where the scar runs depends on how much skin is excess and where. Because the groin is a moist area that moves constantly, healing can be slower there, and Goa’s humidity makes dressing care more important.

Recovery and what to plan

  • Swelling and tightness for several weeks
  • Compression garments and limited arm or leg movement at first
  • Time off work varies, usually one to two weeks for desk jobs
  • Heavy lifting or intense exercise is delayed for around six weeks, as advised
  • Scars mature over twelve months or longer

Weight stability and timing

Surgeons commonly ask for weight to be stable for six months or longer before body contouring. If weight is still changing, the skin will keep changing too, and an operation done too early may need to be repeated. People who have had bariatric surgery are also checked for nutritional deficiencies, such as protein, iron and vitamin B12, because these slow healing.

Some people prefer to do the arms and thighs in separate sessions, since each needs several weeks of reduced movement. Doing both at once is possible in some cases but adds to the load of recovery and is decided individually.

Risks to discuss

These operations carry risks of wound opening, infection, fluid collection, asymmetry, numbness or nerve irritation, widened scars, and a need for revision. Lymph swelling can occur, particularly in the thigh. Smoking and unstable weight raise the risk of problems. Weight gain after surgery can stretch the area again.

For many people, the choice comes down to how much they mind a scar compared with how much they mind the hanging skin. That is a personal decision, and the surgeon should show you where the scar will lie before you decide. Suitability and a written estimate are discussed after examination at consultation.
