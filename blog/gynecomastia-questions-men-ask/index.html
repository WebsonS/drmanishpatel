Gynecomastia: Questions Men Ask Before Surgery

Short answerGynecomastia is enlargement of male breast tissue. It can be gland, fat or both. Fat may reduce with weight loss; firm glandular tissue usually does not. Surgery removes the excess tissue, typically through small incisions near the areola, after medical causes have been checked.

Fat, gland or both?

Some men have soft fullness from fat alone (sometimes called pseudogynecomastia). Others have a firm disc of gland under the nipple. An examination, and occasionally an ultrasound, tells them apart.

Possible causes to rule out first

  • Puberty, where it often settles on its own within two years
  • Certain medicines and anabolic steroids
  • Hormonal, liver, kidney or thyroid conditions
  • Rarely, a lump that needs separate investigation

What surgery involves

Liposuction removes the fatty part, and the gland is cut out through a small incision at the edge of the areola. A compression vest is worn for some weeks. Most men return to desk work within days and to heavy exercise after several weeks, as advised.

Can it come back?

Removed gland does not usually regrow, but weight gain, steroid use or some medicines can cause new enlargement. Risks of surgery include bleeding, fluid collection, contour irregularity, changes in nipple sensation and scarring.
