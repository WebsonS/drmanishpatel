Lip Fillers: What to Know Before Your First Visit

Short answerLip fillers are usually hyaluronic acid gel injected to add volume, define the border or smooth lines. They are temporary, generally lasting several months, and carry risks such as swelling, bruising, lumps, and, rarely, blocked blood vessels. A trained doctor should perform them in a clinical setting.

What a lip filler is

Most lip fillers use hyaluronic acid, a substance that occurs naturally in the skin and holds water. Different products have different firmness and are chosen for different purposes: softer gels for fine lines and subtle volume, firmer ones for definition. Your doctor selects the product and the amount after looking at your lip shape, your face and how your lips move when you talk and smile.

A visit, step by step

  • Discussion of what you want and a check of your medical history, allergies and medicines
  • Photographs for planning and records, taken with consent
  • Cleaning the area and applying numbing cream, and sometimes a local anaesthetic
  • Small injections with a fine needle or blunt cannula
  • Gentle shaping by the doctor and an ice pack afterwards

The whole appointment commonly takes thirty to sixty minutes. You may be advised to avoid alcohol, blood-thinning supplements such as fish oil, and aspirin-type medicines before treatment unless prescribed, since they raise bruising.

What to expect afterwards

Lips are usually swollen for a few days and may look uneven at first. Bruising can occur and may last a week. Firm areas often soften as swelling settles. Avoid pressing or massaging unless instructed, and avoid intense exercise, saunas and very hot food in the first day. If you have an event such as a wedding in Goa’s busy season, leave a few weeks before it so that any swelling and a possible touch-up are not squeezed in at the last minute.

How long it lasts

The gel is slowly absorbed by the body. Many people find results fade over about six to twelve months, although this varies by product, the amount used, your metabolism and how active your lips are. Repeat treatment is needed to maintain the effect. Hyaluronic acid can be dissolved with an enzyme if a result is unwanted or a complication occurs, which is one reason it is the usual choice.

Risks

  • Swelling, bruising, tenderness and asymmetry
  • Lumps, particularly if the product is placed superficially or the lip is overfilled
  • Cold sore flare-ups in people who carry the herpes virus; tell your doctor beforehand
  • Allergic reaction or infection
  • Rarely, blockage of a blood vessel, which can damage skin and, in very rare cases, vision, and needs immediate treatment

Choosing a shape that suits you

Natural lips are rarely symmetrical, and the upper lip is usually a little smaller than the lower. Many people are happier with modest changes than with a large increase, which can look stiff and affect speech and smiling. A cautious doctor may suggest starting small and adding later at a review visit, rather than doing everything at once.

Bring a picture of your own lips when you were happy with them, if you have one, rather than a celebrity photograph. It is a more realistic guide for your face.

Who should inject

Lip anatomy has blood vessels close to the surface, so injecting needs detailed knowledge and the ability to manage complications. Ask who will treat you, what training they have, which brand is being used, and what happens if there is a problem. Opened or unlabelled syringes and treatments at home visits or salons are not advisable.

Suitability is decided at consultation, and a written estimate is provided after assessment.
