Sunscreen and Pigmentation: A Practical Guide for Goa

Short answerUse a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher every morning on exposed skin, apply enough, and reapply every two to three hours when outdoors. For pigmentation, sunscreen is the step that makes other treatments hold, since sun exposure can darken marks and melasma again.

Why Goa is hard on skin

Goa sits close to the coast, and UV radiation stays high through most of the year. Cloud cover in the monsoon filters visible light, but a good share of UV passes through. Water, sand and white buildings reflect more onto the face. Add long hours on two-wheelers, fishing, beach walks or outdoor work, and the skin gets sustained exposure.

Deeper skin tones burn less visibly, which leads many people to believe they do not need protection. In reality UV still triggers pigment production, speeds ageing and carries a risk of skin cancer, though at a lower rate than in fair skin.

Reading the label

  • SPF 30 or above for daily use; higher for beach days or long outdoor work
  • Broad-spectrum or PA+++ (or PA++++) marking for UVA cover, which matters for pigmentation and ageing
  • Water-resistant versions if you sweat heavily or swim, bearing in mind that they still need reapplying
  • Tinted mineral products with iron oxide can help people with melasma, because visible light also darkens pigment

Texture matters more than people admit. A product that feels sticky in humidity will be skipped. Gels, fluids and lightweight lotions are easier to wear in Goa. Try a few until one suits your skin, and patch test if you have a sensitive or acne-prone face.

How much and how often

Most people apply too little. A common guide is about two finger-lengths of cream for the face and neck. Apply it fifteen minutes before going out and reapply every two to three hours when outside, after swimming, and after wiping off sweat. Reapplying over make-up is easier with a powder or spray product, though these are less reliable than cream. Indoors near a window or in a car, protection is still advisable.

Beyond sunscreen

  • Hats, umbrellas, sunglasses and full-sleeved cotton clothing during peak hours
  • Shade during late morning to mid-afternoon
  • Helmet visors and scarves on scooter rides
  • Care with perfumes, certain herbal oils and lime juice on the skin, which can cause a reaction in sun, leaving marks

Common mistakes

Applying sunscreen only once in the morning and expecting it to last through a day outdoors is the most frequent error. Others include skipping the neck, ears, hands and the back of the neck, using an expired product, and relying on the SPF in make-up. Sunscreen kept in a hot car or a scooter dicky can degrade, so store it in a cool place.

Children also need protection. Hats, shade and a gentle product for their age help, and babies under six months should be kept out of direct sun altogether.

Pigmentation and sun protection

Melasma, tan lines, acne marks and dark patches around the neck are all worsened by sun. Treatments such as creams, peels and lasers may lighten pigment for a time, but without regular protection the marks often return. Some conditions, such as melasma, are chronic and relapse easily, and a plan with realistic expectations is better than a quick fix.

See a doctor if a mole changes in size, colour or shape, if a sore does not heal, or if a patch grows quickly. Treatment plans for pigmentation, including their risks, are discussed after examination at consultation, and a written estimate is given afterwards.
