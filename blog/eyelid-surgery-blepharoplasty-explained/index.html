Eyelid Surgery (Blepharoplasty): What to Know

Short answerBlepharoplasty removes or repositions excess skin, fat and sometimes muscle from the upper or lower eyelids. It is done for drooping skin that makes eyes look tired or, in some people, restricts vision. Recovery takes about one to two weeks for visible bruising, and dry eyes and scarring are among the risks.

What eyelid surgery can and cannot change

With age, upper lid skin loosens and may fold over the lash line, while fat can bulge in the lower lids. These changes make some people look tired, or in a few cases they cut into the upper field of vision. Surgery addresses skin and fat. It does not treat a drooping eyebrow, a weak lid muscle (ptosis), or dark circles caused by pigment, and each of those needs a different plan.

Upper and lower lids

  • Upper lid: an incision hidden in the natural lid crease, removing a strip of skin and sometimes a little fat
  • Lower lid: an incision just below the lashes, or inside the lid, to remove or reposition fat bags and tighten skin
  • Combined upper and lower surgery in one sitting, where suitable

Many procedures are done under local anaesthesia with sedation and allow you to go home the same day. Your doctor will explain which approach suits you.

Assessment before surgery

A careful examination checks the position of the brows, lid muscle strength, tear production and how well the eyes close. A person with very dry eyes, thyroid eye disease, or a drooping lid that is not recognised may have problems after surgery. If vision is affected, a visual field test is sometimes done. Tell the surgeon about contact lens use, past eye surgery, glaucoma, high blood pressure, diabetes and any blood thinners.

Recovery you can plan for

  • Swelling and bruising for one to two weeks, and sometimes longer
  • Cold compresses and head elevation during the first days
  • Lubricating eye drops or ointment as prescribed
  • No contact lenses, make-up or strenuous exercise for the period your doctor specifies
  • Stitches are usually removed in about a week
  • Sunglasses outdoors; sun and dust in Goa are especially irritating on newly operated lids

Final appearance of the scars and lid shape takes several months to settle. The upper lid scar tends to hide well within the crease; a lower scar is usually fine and close to the lashes.

Asymmetry and other surprises

Most faces are not perfectly even, and eyelids are no exception. One brow may sit higher than the other, or one eye may be slightly more hooded. Surgery can narrow the difference but rarely erases it. Photographs taken before surgery help the surgeon point out these differences to you, so that they do not seem like new problems afterwards.

Tell the surgeon if you sleep with your eyes slightly open, if your eyes water easily, or if your eyes feel gritty in air-conditioning. These are small clues to dryness that may become more noticeable after the operation.

Risks

Risks include temporary or lasting dry eye, asymmetry, difficulty closing the eyes fully, a lower lid pulled downward (ectropion), scarring, bleeding behind the eye, and, very rarely, loss of vision. Over-removal of skin cannot easily be reversed, so careful surgeons tend to be conservative.

Eyelid surgery is often combined with other procedures such as brow lifting. Whether it suits you is decided at examination by Dr. Patel, and a written estimate is given after consultation.
