Dark Circles Under the Eyes: Causes and Options

Short answerUnder-eye dark circles can be caused by extra pigment, thin skin showing blood vessels, hollows that cast shadows, or puffy fat bags. Sleep and screen time are only part of the story, and the right treatment, whether creams, peels, fillers or surgery, depends on which cause applies to you.

Four common causes

Many people assume dark circles mean a lack of sleep. Poor sleep can make them more visible, but the underlying reasons are usually structural or inherited.

  • Pigmentation: extra melanin in the skin, common in Indian skin tones and often run in families
  • Thin, translucent skin that shows blood vessels beneath, giving a bluish or purplish tint
  • Hollows at the tear trough, where a groove casts a shadow
  • Puffy lower lids, where fat bulges and shadows form beneath them

Allergies, rubbing the eyes, eczema, sun exposure, iron deficiency and some medicines can add to the problem. Long hours on the phone are not a proven cause but can encourage rubbing and poor sleep.

A simple home test

In front of a mirror, gently stretch the skin sideways. If the colour stays, pigment is likely a large factor. If it fades, the colour comes from blood vessels or thin skin. Now look with a torch held from above and then from below. If the dark area changes or disappears when the light angle changes, the cause is shadow from a hollow or bulge. This is only a rough guide, and a doctor’s examination is more reliable.

Options matched to the cause

  • Pigment: sun protection every day, prescribed lightening creams, and selected peels or lasers, used carefully because the skin around the eyes is delicate and can darken if irritated
  • Thin skin: skin-care routines with ingredients recommended by your doctor, and sometimes treatments that stimulate collagen
  • Hollows: hyaluronic acid filler placed by an experienced doctor, which is temporary and carries risks of swelling, lumps and rarely vascular problems
  • Bags and puffiness: lower eyelid surgery in people who are suitable

Often more than one cause is present, so the plan combines steps and takes time. Pigment treatments usually need months, and sun exposure can undo progress, which matters in a coastal state like Goa where UV levels are high most of the year, even on cloudy days.

What does not help much

Cucumber slices, tea bags and a long list of home remedies may reduce puffiness for a short while but will not change pigment or structure. Strong bleaching products bought without advice can irritate the skin and make the area darker. Please check with a doctor before using steroid-containing creams around the eyes, as they can damage the skin and raise eye pressure.

Everyday habits that help a little

Sleep, drinking enough water, and treating allergies and nasal congestion can reduce puffiness and the habit of rubbing the eyes. A cold compress in the morning may reduce swelling for a short while. Wearing sunglasses on the road and using sunscreen on the cheekbone area protects the thin skin there.

None of these changes the underlying structure, so if the circles are a family trait, expect only a small difference. This is worth knowing before spending money on products that promise more.

When to be examined

See a doctor if circles appeared suddenly, are one-sided, come with swelling or itching, or you also feel unusually tired or look pale, since blood tests may be needed. Treatment choices, their risks and limits, and the likelihood of improvement are discussed at consultation. A written estimate follows examination, and no outcome can be promised.
